# Architecture & Repository Boundaries

The Accensa ecosystem spans three repositories. Each has a distinct role, and understanding the boundaries between them is essential for integrators and reviewers alike.

## The Three Repositories

### `accensa-app` — Merchant Back-Office (this repository)

A Next.js dashboard and indexer that sits **downstream** of a facilitator. It reconstructs payment history from on-chain data, attributes settlements to HTTP routes (via the middleware hook), anchors verifiable receipts, and executes merchant-authorized refunds.

**What it does:**

- Indexes Stellar Asset Contract `transfer` events into a local Postgres ledger
- Attributes payments to HTTP routes (when the SDK hook is installed)
- Aggregates revenue by route, method, and time window
- Anchors batch Merkle roots to the `ReceiptAnchor` Soroban contract
- Provides a dashboard for reconciliation, refunds, and receipt verification

**What it does not do:**

- Process x402 payments — that is the facilitator's job
- Escrow or move funds — payments flow directly from buyer to merchant
- Verify x402 payment payloads against Soroban auth entries

### `accensa-contracts` — On-Chain Trust Layer

Soroban smart contracts (`ReceiptAnchor`, `RefundVault`) that provide the on-chain source of truth for verifiable receipts and trustless refunds.

**What it does:**

- Stores batch Merkle roots on-chain for receipt verification
- Manages a refund vault for trustless merchant-initiated refunds

**What it does not do:**

- Process or verify x402 payments — it has no knowledge of the x402 protocol
- Handle off-chain business logic

### `x402-facilitator-stellar` — x402 Payment Facilitator

The standard HTTP 402 facilitator that handles the x402 payment flow, indexes resource prices, and processes Stellar payments from AI agents.

**What it does:**

- Verifies x402 payment payloads against Soroban auth entries
- Processes the x402 payment flow end to end
- Dispatches webhooks after payment verification

**What it does not do:**

- Index payment history — that is the dashboard's job
- Anchor receipts or manage refunds — that is the contracts' job

## Relationship to the Dashboard

The `accensa-app` dashboard is a **merchant back-office** that sits downstream of a facilitator. It does not process payments itself. A merchant runs both a facilitator (to accept payments) and the Accensa dashboard (to manage the business operations around those payments). The dashboard reads on-chain data and, optionally, receives route attribution from the facilitator via the SDK hook.

## The `/verify` Naming Collision

Both `accensa-app` and `x402-facilitator-stellar` expose a `/verify` endpoint. These are **different operations that happen to share a word.**

**Receipt verification** (`accensa-app` `POST /api/verify`) verifies that a specific payment was included in a batch Merkle root anchored on-chain. It takes a batch ID, a leaf hash, and a Merkle proof, then checks the proof both locally and against the `ReceiptAnchor` contract. This is what an agent operator uses to confirm a merchant's receipt is legitimate.

**x402 payment verification** (`x402-facilitator-stellar`) verifies a payment payload against Soroban auth entries. It confirms that a Stellar transaction actually paid the invoice defined by the x402 protocol. This happens at payment time, before the dashboard is involved at all.

The two share the word "verify" but operate on different data, at different times, for different purposes. Receipt verification checks historical proof of inclusion; x402 payment verification checks that a payment was valid when it happened.

## How Data Flows

```
AI Agent → x402 Facilitator → Stellar Network
                                  ↓
                          Accensa Indexer (reads on-chain transfers)
                                  ↓
                          Accensa Dashboard (revenue, receipts, refunds)
                                  ↓
                          ReceiptAnchor Contract (batch Merkle roots)
```

The facilitator handles the payment. The indexer observes the result on-chain. The dashboard presents it. The contracts anchor it. No component oversteps its boundary.
